Why the Smaller List Exists

A new player packet should not try to present every possible valid investigator concept at the same level of emphasis. The smaller list in this packet is meant to show a stable set of strong entry lanes into FRH: people who can investigate, contribute, survive pressure, and become legible inside the setting without the setting having to bend around them too abruptly.

That is not the same thing as saying FRH only supports a narrow kind of character. It means the front door benefits from focus.

What the Smaller Set Is For

Think of the smaller NPP set as the first shelf, not the whole library. Those investigators are there to demonstrate the campaign's most immediately readable lanes: field competence, occult perception, social access, disciplined force, dangerous scholarship, and the way burden changes from character to character. They make it easier to see how FRH works before the setting starts showing off everything else it can hold.

The Missing Character Type Idea

One useful way to test a setting is to ask what kinds of characters seem "missing" at first glance and then see whether the setting can still absorb them without losing its identity. FRH performs surprisingly well under that kind of pressure. It can handle a wider range of investigators, companions, operators, touched souls, survivors, occult specialists, and burdened oddities than a casual summary might suggest.

The important limit is not whether a concept is conventional. The important limit is whether it can become real inside FRH's social, historical, and occult logic. The setting is broad. It is not shapeless.

Use the Sample-Character Site As the Wider Demonstration

If you want to see how far FRH can stretch while still feeling like itself, use the larger sample-character archive. That site shows many more valid character lanes than this packet needs to foreground. Some are easy first-pass entry points. Some are advanced, dangerous, or eccentric. Together they demonstrate that FRH is not a brittle genre prison.

What This Means for Your Character

  • You do not need to make the one "correct" kind of occult investigator.
  • You do need to make someone who can live in a dangerous, secretive, socially grounded world.
  • Range is a strength of FRH, but coherence matters more than novelty.
  • If a concept is unusual, the question is not "Is this allowed?" so much as "How does this become real here?"
