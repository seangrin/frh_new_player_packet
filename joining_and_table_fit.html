Joining and Table Fit

FRH asks a lot of its players, but not in the form of perfection. What it rewards most is curiosity, attention, generosity toward the group, and a willingness to become more interesting under pressure instead of shutting down under it.

Who Usually Thrives Here

Players who tend to do well in FRH usually like mysteries, half-seen structures, awkward conversations, old documents, pressure-filled scenes, and the feeling that a problem may be social, practical, moral, and supernatural all at once. They do not need to be the loudest people at the table. They do not need prior GURPS experience. What helps most is that they care about noticing things, asking good questions, and giving the group something useful to work with.

Some players arrive already comfortable with roleplaying. Others arrive hesitant, analytical, shy, or a little embarrassed. FRH can support all of those as long as the player wants to engage. The game has room for learning in public. It has room for someone who begins by wanting to help and becomes central. It has room for someone who arrives with rough instincts and grows into a real investigator over time.

If You Want the Rules First

You do not need to learn all of GURPS before you join. But if you are the sort of player who feels better after seeing the bones of the system, GURPS Lite, Third Edition is the best lightweight starting point. It is a free PDF and much less intimidating than trying to infer the whole game from scattered references.

If you would rather see how the rules feel in motion than read them in the abstract, the Tiberius example play-through shows an FRH investigator using stealth, psychic powers, and ordinary tactical judgment in a dangerous scene. It is one of the quickest ways to get a feel for how the game actually breathes at the table.

What the Table Is Like

This is an adult table. The game is serious, but not solemn every minute. People joke, recover, question things, misread situations, and then suddenly find themselves in scenes that matter a great deal. That rhythm is part of the life of the game. If you enjoy collaborative play that makes room for both tension and humanity, you are likely closer to the intended fit than someone who only wants airtight tactical control.

What We Hope You Bring

  • Curiosity about strange problems.
  • Respect for other players' spotlight and weirdness.
  • Interest in making a person, not just a build.
  • Comfort with not knowing everything at the start.
  • Willingness to let danger matter without treating danger as the only pleasure available.

Practical Notes

Sessions are expected to run roughly once a month on Saturday evenings, with a hybrid setup when needed. Adult schedules are understood. Characters can be called away on other work when a player is absent. The campaign is built to keep moving without treating attendance friction as moral failure.

If this sounds like the kind of game you would enjoy, the best next step is simple: think about the kind of investigator you would want to be, the kind of mystery that would draw them in, and the kind of burden that would make them belong in this world.
