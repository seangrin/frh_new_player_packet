How FRH Plays

FRH is an investigation game first, but not a passive one. You are not there to watch the mystery unfold. You are there to notice, test, decide, and live with what those decisions reveal.

The Usual Mission Loop

Most FRH sessions begin with a report, a rumor, a letter, a vision, an artifact, or some other disturbance that demands attention. Sometimes the cause is fraud. Sometimes it is human cruelty wearing a theatrical mask. Sometimes it is genuinely supernatural. Part of the pleasure of the game is that the investigators do not know which kind of case they have yet.

From there, play usually turns on approach. Who do you talk to first? What records matter? What institution might help or hinder? What can be learned by examining a place, a body, a ledger, or an object? What does your own investigator notice that the others do not? A good FRH session often contains witness interviews, library work, practical fieldcraft, social maneuvering, theory-building, and one or two moments where everyone realizes the case is larger than it first looked.

What Matters In Play

Research

Old newspapers, registries, church records, legal histories, shipping manifests, rumor chains, and personal papers can all matter.

Observation

Small details often decide what kind of problem you are really facing. The game rewards attention.

Social Pressure

A conversation with a senator's son, a priest, a criminal broker, or a frightened witness can change the shape of the whole case.

Different Clue Vectors

Each investigator tends to create a different path into the truth. One notices motives, another history, another danger, another logistics, another the hidden spiritual pressure in the room.

Understanding Beats Brute Force More Often Than You Might Think

FRH is not a game where every problem is solved by violence, and it is not a game where violence never matters. The point is that knowing what you are dealing with usually changes what “winning” even means. Some threats have rules, habits, weaknesses, obligations, or blind spots that only become visible through investigation. Some cases are best resolved by preparation, leverage, deception, or restraint. Even when a fight breaks out, the groundwork that led there usually matters more than raw damage output.

Why It Feels Different In Practice

FRH is built for players who enjoy acting under uncertainty. The world does not explain itself all at once, and the investigators are not meant to stand outside it with perfect safety. That does not mean the game is hostile. It means the fun comes from becoming more legible to one another and to the case as pressure builds. People joke, recover, argue, misread, notice, improvise, and then suddenly realize the room has shifted around them.

The story is not prewritten. The Game Master provides the world, the people in it, and the consequences of what the investigators choose to do. The shape of the campaign emerges from those choices, from the theories the investigators pursue, and from the things they are brave or foolish enough to touch.

What You Do Not Need

You do not need prior GURPS experience. You do not need to arrive as a perfect tactician or a polished actor. What helps most is curiosity, attention, willingness to contribute, and a taste for strange problems that may not yield neat answers on the first pass.

If you are curious about the rules, the simplest place to start is GURPS Lite, Third Edition, a free introductory PDF that gives you the feel of the system without asking you to learn everything at once.
