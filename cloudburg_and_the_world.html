Why Cloudburg Matters

Cloudburg, Colorado is not simply a spooky town. It is a mining county seat, a university town, a rail stop, a social hierarchy, and a chapter house site. It has laboring districts, respectable districts, institutional power, local folklore, academic curiosity, and the quiet knowledge that some parts of the land should be left alone after dark. In other words, it feels like a place where a case can begin naturally and then keep unfolding outward.

The town is built on a slope, and status follows elevation. Workers, ore, freight, and the rail line dominate the lower district. Banks, trustees, and the town's decision-makers occupy the middle. The university pulls ideas and outside influence into the upper layers. Edgar's house sits apart from all of them just enough to feel intentional. That social geography matters because FRH mysteries rarely float free of class, labor, respectability, or ownership. The supernatural enters a world that already has pressure in it.

The Larger World

FRH is set in 1923, and that date matters. The country is modern enough for trains, telegraphs, bank wires, newspapers, insurance offices, laboratories, and organized criminal networks to shape daily life. It is also old enough for occult societies, private scholars, provincial power brokers, inherited grudges, and local legends to remain fully plausible parts of the same social fabric. The game lives in that overlap.

This means an FRH case can move from a library table to a train compartment, from a church office to a foundry floor, from a hotel lobby to a dream-laboratory, without feeling like it has left the world behind. The weird does not replace the ordinary. It enters it, pressures it, and reveals that the ordinary world had more hidden seams than most people realized.

What the Setting Feels Like

Sky, rail, dust, paper, hotel brass, church stone, old lake water, distant freight, expensive coffee, bad whiskey, hidden sub-basements, careful letters, and the sensation that social life is only half the story. FRH should feel grounded enough that a witness statement matters and strange enough that an apple tree on an island or a sealed chapter-house relic can quietly reorder the whole conversation.
